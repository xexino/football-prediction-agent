# Agent 11 — Competitive Intelligence and Growth Strategist

## 1. Current situation
Football Intelligence Lab (FIL) is a public, free repository with a static GitHub Pages dashboard. It cannot compete with SofaScore, FotMob or Flashscore on live scores, coverage breadth, player ratings or event data. Those products are built on licensed live feeds, and FIL has no live or lineup source (README.md:28-29). FIL's realistic advantage is **verifiable honesty**: immutable predictions, NO BET as the default outcome, market and baseline comparisons, two independent engines, and coverage status labelled per competition. The infrastructure for this exists. The *public, externally verifiable track record* does not exist yet, and the repository has none of the basics needed for discovery.

Facts measured on 2026-11-23:
* The production `predictions` table holds 121 rows, all created between 2026-11-22 21:22 and 23:40 UTC. All 121 are `NO_BET` and 0 are evaluated. `eb_predictions` holds 130 rows. `virtual_bets` and `eb_bets` hold 0 rows each, and `eb_validation_runs` holds 0 (prod.db queries). The public track record is therefore **less than one day old and empty**.
* GitHub repo metadata (`gh api repos/xexino/football-prediction-agent`): `description:null, topics:[], license:null, homepage:null, stars:0, forks:0`. There is no LICENSE file at the repository root (`ls -a`).
* Region eligibility (from `competitions`): Europe 31/34 eligible, Americas 4/9, Asia 2/6, Africa 0/8, International 0/6.

## 2. Evidence
**Repository and product**
* Immutability is enforced by SQLite triggers: `trg_predictions_noupdate` and `trg_predictions_nodelete` (sqlite_master), `backend/fil/db.py:8`, with revisions stored as new rows (`db.py:265-267`) and `inputs_hash` (`db.py:278`).
* The public site is published by `git init` plus `git push -f` to gh-pages on every run (`.github/workflows/autopilot.yml:128-137`, comment "replaced on every run"). **The public history is overwritten each time.** The DB is `--clobber`-uploaded to `db-latest`, and only 14 daily backups are kept (autopilot.yml:113-119). The triggers protect against the app, but the owner can still change history. A third party cannot prove that a prediction existed before kickoff.
* The export caps the ledger at `predictions(limit=2000)` (`backend/fil/cli.py:130`). Older history will silently fall out of the public JSON.
* An A-vs-B-vs-market comparison already exists: `/data/engine_compare.json` has per-fixture `engine_a`, `engine_b`, `market` and `disagreement`. `/data/analytics.json` has a `calibration` array, currently empty.
* An honest coverage label exists: `/data/insights.json` shows the Tunisian Ligue 1 fixture as "COVERAGE ONLY — NOT ENOUGH DATA FOR PREDICTIONS". `/data/coverage_matrix.json` marks MAR1, CAFCL and EGY1 "awaiting API_FOOTBALL_KEY".
* A simulation banner exists: `frontend/src/main.tsx:66`. Weekly reports exist in the DB (`reports` kind=weekly, 679 chars, 2026-11-22) and are exported to `/data/reports.json`, but they are not published anywhere people follow.
* The claimed walk-forward results (brief: A 0.9949, market 0.9819, naive 1.0768) say the model beats the naive baseline but not the market. Publishing this is a credibility asset, not a weakness.

**Public competitor features (official pages, no claims about internals)**
* Opta Analyst's "supercomputer" publishes season simulations. Its own page says match probabilities use "betting market odds and the Opta Power Rankings" and that remaining fixtures are simulated thousands of times. It also publishes retrospective reviews of its pre-season tables ([theanalyst.com/articles/opta-football-predictions](https://theanalyst.com/articles/opta-football-predictions), [2023-24 review](https://theanalyst.com/articles/opta-supercomputer-premier-league-2023-24-predictions/)). These reviews are self-selected articles, not a scored ledger.
* FiveThirtyEight published SPI match forecasts as open CSVs and a methodology page ([methodology](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/how-our-club-soccer-predictions-work), [data repo](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/tree/master/soccer-spi)). The live feed has stopped updating (third-party report: [GitHub issue #332](https://www.github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/issues/332)). Its current status is UNKNOWN from official sources. Open forecasts that can be downloaded and scored have no maintained successor, and this is a gap FIL can fill.
* ClubElo offers free historical Elo through a CSV API back to 1939 (documented by the third-party wrapper [soccerdata](https://soccerdata.readthedocs.io/en/latest/datasources/ClubElo.html)). It is a free external benchmark FIL can score against.
* SofaScore Attack Momentum is a live graph built from shots, dangerous passes and set pieces ([sofascore.com/news](https://www.sofascore.com/news/how-sofascores-attack-momentum-changed-sport-analysis)). FotMob player ratings are "based on more than 300 individual stats… from Opta", and FotMob shows live xG in 23 competitions ([fotmob.com/faq](https://fotmob.com/faq), [live xG note](https://www.fotmob.com/topnews/3627-live-xg-data-is-now-fotmob)). Flashscore's store listing claims 6,000+ competitions with xG and ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/flashscore/id766443283)). These are what users expect from a live app, and FIL should **not** try to match them.
* Understat covers xG for 6 leagues. A third-party roundup says FBref removed its Opta advanced stats in January 2026 ([originalxg blog](https://originalxg.pages.dev/blog/free-football-data-sources)). This is UNVERIFIED and should be confirmed on fbref.com. If it is true, free public xG is getting scarcer.
* Tipster sites advertise "verified" picks with no published verification method (Blogabet's self-claim, reported via a review snapshot). Credible records need pre-event timestamps and complete archives including losses, as argued in this analysis: [startuphub.ai](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/prediction-markets/2026/verifying-prediction-market-track-records-what-s-real). Some services use blockchain sealing (e.g. Agatha.gg, per [ambcrypto](https://ambcrypto.com/ai-sports-prediction-model-agatha-gg-surpasses-1000-graded-picks/)). FIL can do the same thing for free with git commits or OpenTimestamps.

**Legal and ethical context**
* ASA/CAP (UK) rules say organic social posts can count as gambling ads and must not strongly appeal to under-18s ([ASA: "Odds are your gambling post is an ad"](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/odds-are-your-gambling-post-is-an-ad.html), [under-18 enforcement notice](https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/enforcement-notice-gambling-ads-with-strong-appeal-to-under-18s.html)). FIL is not an operator and has no affiliate links. Even so, posts that show bookmaker odds and "value bets" could look like gambling promotion. Italy (the dashboard uses Rome time) has a broad gambling-advertising ban (Decreto Dignità). Legal applicability to a non-commercial open-source project is UNKNOWN, and the owner should seek advice before running promotional social channels.

## 3. What is working well
* The rules, mechanics and data behind honest credibility already exist: triggers, revisions as new rows, inputs hashes, NO BET as default, CLV fields, market and naive baselines, a coverage-status vocabulary, and a simulation banner.
* The two-engine comparison with market and disagreement is unusual among free products, and it is already exported.
* The README states plainly that the model "does not beat the market" (README.md:21). This is rare in this category and an asset worth keeping.

## 4. What is weak or missing
* There is no third-party-verifiable pre-registration. gh-pages history is wiped and the DB is overwritten.
* There is no public scored track record yet: 0 evaluated predictions, an empty calibration array, and 0 validation runs in production.
* There is no discoverability: no description, topics, license, homepage or social preview. Without a LICENSE the project is not legally open source, so others cannot reuse or contribute.
* Weekly reports are not distributed: there is no RSS, no Markdown-in-repo, and no GitHub Discussions or Release notes.
* The ledger export is capped at 2,000 predictions, with no downloadable full CSV or Parquet.
* There is no external benchmark beyond the market: no ClubElo-implied or naive scoring column in the public ledger.

## 5. Important risks
* **Credibility risk**: an "immutable" claim that outsiders cannot check is no different from a tipster's claim.
* **Regulatory and reputational risk**: social posts of "value bets" with bookmaker odds could be read as gambling promotion. Mitigation: publish probabilities and scores, not "tips". Show no bookmaker branding or links. Add an 18+ / simulation note and a link to a gambling-help resource.
* **Data-licence risk**: football-data.co.uk odds and results are redistributed in the public DB release. Their terms of reuse are UNKNOWN to me and should be checked before promoting downloads widely.
* **Small-sample risk**: early ROI or calibration numbers will be noisy. Posts must show confidence intervals and sample sizes, or they will mislead.

## 7. Top 3 recommendations
1. **GR-1, a verifiable pre-registration ledger.** This is the only differentiator that no live-score app or tipster site in this review offers for free: proof, checkable by anyone, that each forecast existed before kickoff. Opta publishes self-selected retrospectives, and tipster sites assert "verified" without a published method. FIL already computes `inputs_hash` and enforces immutability locally. The only missing piece is an external timestamp, and the current force-push publish step destroys exactly that evidence. The change is small, cheap, and can be checked with a script.
2. **GR-2, a public scorecard against the market, a naive baseline and ClubElo, plus the weekly report.** FiveThirtyEight-style open forecasts that can be downloaded and scored have no maintained successor. A weekly, reproducible scorecard that openly says "we do not beat the market yet", with CIs, gives people a reason to return and gives FIL its content channel without any gambling framing. It becomes meaningful after a few hundred settled fixtures, so it should be switched on now so the clock starts.
3. **GR-3 together with GR-7: licence, repository metadata and a content policy.** Without a LICENSE the project is not open source in law, and without a description or topics it cannot be found. This is an XS fix. The content policy protects the project's main asset, its integrity, from being read as gambling promotion under ASA/CAP-style rules once posting begins.
